# Using the space: fluid first, no voids, no clipping (owner, 2026-10-13)

Suggestions, not laws. Each one has a reason, and a good reason to break it wins. They apply the same way in the browser and in Figma. The Figma mechanics are in [ext/Figma/doc/figma-layout.md](/framework/ext/Figma/doc/figma-layout.md).

- **Comfortable reading width (owner, 2026-10-13).** A line of body text stays around 60–80 characters: about 40em, or 640–760 px at 16–18 px type (the site's `--measure`). No paragraph runs the full width of a wide card or page. On a wide surface, divide it (two columns, or text beside a specimen) instead of stretching the text. Long content gets chunked: a heading per topic, short items, one idea per line. A 30-line paragraph is a failure, not a summary.
- **Strong margins (owner, 2026-10-13).** A page looks strong when its items keep hitting the same left and right edges, plus the centre line in a split: text, cards, images and headings alike. Left-aligned blocks that each end at a different right edge look weak. Set the margins with padding *and* a max width (centred), not padding alone: padding that is right at 1920 is too big on a phone, and full width at 1920 with a little padding makes lines far too long to read.
- **Fill the width, then divide it.** When content doesn't need the full width, don't leave it short. Put it in a card or band that reaches both margins, and divide the inside (centre it, or split it into two columns). The outer edge keeps the margins strong and the inside sets a good reading width.
- **Hierarchy is mostly nesting grounds.** An item on a card on a section reads as "belongs to". Use it, but don't overdo it: boxes inside boxes inside boxes stack up borders and overwhelm. Some things read better unboxed, such as a file tree, a plain list, or rows separated by space alone.
- **Count-driven widths.** Three items set to fill each take about a third. Add a fourth and all of them shrink, which is fine for a robust card and bad for one that can't shrink (icons wrap, titles break). Give such cards a min width, so the row wraps instead.
- **Content length changes how a card responds.** A short title beside an icon survives a lot of narrowing; a long one wraps the icon onto its own line much sooner. Test each card with its longest real title at its narrowest width.
- **Fluid by default.** A card, a column or a section fills the width it is given and grows to the height its content needs. Fix a size only where consistency is the point: an icon, an avatar, a control's hit area, or a column you deliberately pin (for example a 280 sidebar next to a fluid main). Fixed widths leave voids when there is more room and clip when there is less.
- **Never clip content to fit a box.** A box that cuts off its own progress bar or last line is broken, not tidy. Let it grow, or give the content less to show.
- **Fixed + fluid is a normal pair.** Not every column needs to be equal. A pinned narrow column beside a filling one usually reads better than two equal halves.
- **Avoid big voids.** Some white space separates groups, and that is good. A row of three fixed cards with half the width empty beside them reads as broken. Let the cards fill, add a column, or change the layout.
- **Repetition reads as order.** A row of equal-height cards is calm, and ragged heights in a row look accidental. When heights must vary, use a masonry: columns balanced by real height (on the web, [ext/sprawl](/framework/ext/sprawl/)), not a grid with holes.
- **Gaps under a grid look broken (owner, 2026-10-13).** Cards of different heights in a plain grid leave ragged holes under the shorter ones, and the eye reads that as broken instantly. Pick one of three: equal heights, a masonry (columns balanced by height, so there are no holes), or content kept to the same length. Don't leave a ragged grid.
- **Equal height means top-aligned content.** When cards share a height, align their content to the top, not the middle. Centring a two-line, a three-line and a four-line paragraph makes their tops wave up and down. A top-aligned card with some space at the bottom looks normal, because readers are used to that.
- **Content length is a design input.** Cards meant to sit side by side read best when their text is about the same length. Write to the layout when you can; switch to a masonry when you can't.
- **Rows of one page share one width.** If sections or rows on a page end at different right edges, something has a fixed width. Let the page fill, then each section, then each item.
- **Wrapping grids: min and max widths decide the count.** Give a card a min width (where it wraps) and a max width (where it stops growing), and let it fill between them. The container then shows 4-up, 3-up, 2-up or 1-up on its own.
- **Orphans.** When a grid reflows, the last row may not fill. Eight items are two rows of 4, but 3 + 3 + 2 at 3-up. The last row either leaves a gap (a *small orphan*, the cheap and often fine choice) or stretches its items wider (a *big orphan*, which reads as deliberate when it is). Six items divide well (3, 2 and 1-up), and 12 better still. Pick the count with this in mind when the content allows it, but never cut real content to suit the grid.
- **Simplest layouts first.** Show a single column and a two-column split before anything clever. Most pages are one of those.
- **Align when you can.** Shared edges (left edges, tops of cards, baselines) make a page look designed even when it is plain.
- **Two-column sections are a strong default at 1920 and up:** a narrow left column for the title and a short description of the part, with the content filling the right. See [/framework/design/layout/](/framework/design/layout/) for the approved layouts.
