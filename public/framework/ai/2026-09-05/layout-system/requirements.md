## Deliverables (numbered; tick each at the end)

1. **`/imagine/layouts/`** — a new realm (the mastermind registers it in `/imagine/page.js`; write its `page.js`). It opens with the plain sentence that says what a layout is here and the one idea underneath everything: **a STACK is what a div does on its own (things follow each other down the page); a SPLIT divides a fixed area into pieces (by %, by fixed sizes, by flex-grow/basis, by fr tracks).** Show the two side by side, live, with the same three boxes: stacked, then split. Then the numbering rule in one sentence, then the catalogue.
2. **The numbered system.** `1.*` one column · `2.*` two columns · `3.*` three columns · `4.*` four or more. Under each number, the permutations as pages you can click through: **distribution** (equal; golden 61.8/38.2; main-aside; rail-main-aside; fixed + fluid; fr tracks — use the words `styles/layouts/cols/` already has, link them), **padding** (the spacing clamps: tight / default / airy — tokens, never constants), **bg color** (the five surfaces, and color permuted WITH layout: every layout page has a surface chip row so a reader sees `2.golden` on plain, card, tint, prim, dark without a new page), **navigation type** (none · crumbs · top tabs · left rail · right rail · bottom bar — from `ext/tabs`, `ext/layout`, `ext/drawer`, paging's toolbar). Not the cross product as pages: one page per distribution, with chips for padding, surface and navigation; the url remembers nothing (rule 4 of the night — a reset chip instead).
3. **The 3-column card technique**, as the way every catalogue entry is shown: a large card (works at 3440, any height) with three columns — LEFT a small title, a two-sentence intro, the chips; CENTER the layout itself, live, as a responsive viewport (a box whose width the reader can set to 400 / 1280 / 1920 / 3440-equivalent with a chip, scaled to fit); RIGHT the readouts: the CSS the layout is (four lines), the measured track widths at the current viewport, and the one line of config that makes a page from it. Several cards on one page are RELATED — scrolling from `2.equal` to `2.golden` to `2.main-aside`, the reader sees the same content re-divided. Build the card once (a class, parts as static subclasses) and use it for every entry.
4. **Rows inside columns, and scroll sections.** In `3.*` and `4.*`, at least two entries split a column into rows (a 2×2, a 3-up with a stacked aside), and one uses scroll sections (a column that scrolls in snapping sections while the others stay). Say in a sentence when each is the right answer.
5. **Takeover with persistent navigation.** A `full` page loses the row, and at 3440 the crumb strip is a thin line far away. Build the `full` variant of the catalogue: every layout can be opened full-screen, and full-screen keeps a persistent way to move to the next/previous layout and back (a small bar or rail that is part of the layout, not the crumb strip — the reader clicks through many full-screen layouts without leaving full). Then answer the owner's question in the page: are the crumbs always there? Give the reader a chip to hide the crumb strip on a full page, and say what the way back is when they are hidden.
6. **Docs:** `readme.md` (the index), `doc/decisions.md` (why numbered, why chips not pages, the relation to the approved five and to `styles/layouts/`).
