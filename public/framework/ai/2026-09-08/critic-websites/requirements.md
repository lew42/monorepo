## Judge — six questions, each answered with evidence, then fixed

1. **The newcomer test.** Open `/websites/`, three site pages (a docs site, a marketing site, a news site — pick from `site/index.json`), one tag page, `/websites/patterns/` at 400 and 1920 headless (a 20-line probe in the scratchpad, `critic-websites-*`; screenshots into this dir; Read them). Can a stranger say what each page is for in ten seconds? Is level 1 one screen? On a site page, do the four real shots and the three wires under them tell the responsive story at a glance, or does something have to be read first? Fix the pages.
2. **The records, sampled.** Pick eight records across categories. For each, LOOK at the four shots and check the hand half: is `layout` at each width right (an id a stranger would agree with)? are `sections` real and in reading order? is `notes` a paragraph a five-year-old follows? Fix what is wrong through `tools/lib.mjs`'s merge (`read_record` / `write_record`), only the hand-half keys `layout`, `sections`, `tags`, `notes`, `responsive.strategy`; never `wire`, never the machine half. Log every change with the site and why.
3. **Tag hygiene, across all 47.** Read `index.json`'s `tags`. Merge synonyms into one spelling (`centred at 1920` / `max-width`, `3 column grid` / `3-column grid`, `hamburger at 940` / `hamburger 940`), lowercase everything, and decide which tags are about layout, design or responsiveness (keep) and which are capture notes (`bot wall`, `cookie modal` — keep them out of the tag row and in `notes`, or keep them as tags if a reader would click them: decide, say why). The patterns author found the corpus spells its docs category three ways and that records still write the retired `3-cards` and `4-equal` (`layouts.json` aliases them to `3-equal` and `n-wall`) — fix the records to the canonical id and one spelling per category. Apply through the merge over every record it touches, re-run `index.mjs`, and write `doc/tags.md`: the vocabulary the corpus uses, one line each, grouped (layout ids · technique · traits · behaviour · category). `/layouts/` defines the layout words; link it rather than redefining.
4. **The numbers.** The headline on `/websites/patterns/` agrees with a count you run yourself in Node over `site/index.json`; print both in your log. The bars are readable at 400.
5. **The tool gap.** `scan.mjs` counted zero stylesheets and zero media queries on privy.io because its CSS is inline `<style>` text (286 KB). Make the scan read inline style text for `@media` / `@container` too; prove it against a local page on `:8123` that has inline style (find one, or `/websites/` itself), not against the web. Note it in `doc/tools.md`.
6. **Two records nobody finished.** `django-docs-index` is the only record with no `wire` — look at its 1920 and 400 shots and write one (`doc/wire.md`; it is a `2-sidebar` docs page). `python-docs-index` claims five shots but only `1280.jpg` and `1920.jpg` exist in `site/python-docs-index/` (the crawl was cut off, and nothing may be re-shot tonight): make `Site.js` draw a missing shot as a plain placeholder that says "not captured" at that width — a record must never show a broken image — and write its wire from the two shots it has. Then `index.mjs`.
7. **The blog leftovers.** `public/blog/readme.md` needs one Watch out line: `image:` in `posts.js` must be a PNG — `meta.mjs` reads an IHDR for the size and a .jpg card ships no width or height (the blog author found it, its fence stopped it). `public/imagine/research/page.js`'s `question:` sentence still says four topics; make it true (five: four in ancient technology, one on browser automation). Open `/blog/ai/playwright/` at 400 and 1920 and apply the newcomer test to its first screen; fix only what fails it.
