## Questions — a closed list

1. **Prior art, one entry each, with the published rules linked:** Stack Overflow privileges by reputation threshold (and its decline — what went wrong), Reddit karma + subreddit mod tooling + the 2023 mod revolt, Wikipedia's governance (RfA, ArbCom, notability, the deletionist wars), Discord roles/permissions, Duolingo and Khan Academy progression (streaks, mastery, what is gamed), university numbering (100/200/300, prerequisites), Codecademy/freeCodeCamp certificates and their labour-market value, Scouts/martial-arts belts (a non-school ladder that works).
2. **Levels.** Compare 1–3 / 1–5 / 1–10 / academic hundreds / organic. What a level must MEAN to be portable across topics without being uniform (a rubric: what you can do, not hours spent). Automatic tracking: which signals are honest (completed intros, accepted contributions, answers marked helpful, time-on-task is NOT). Level 1 as an accomplishment; the 10-minute intro awarding nothing. Invisible during browsing — where it shows and where it never does.
3. **Reputation.** Topic-scoped, derived never stored; which actions earn it and which must not (popularity alone, volume); decay or not; how it gates creation privileges (subtopics, spaces, moderation); the "Anonymous — Level 5 JavaScript" display — what it communicates and what it risks (the users topic covers deanonymization; you cover the social effect).
4. **Governance and moderation.** Owner authority as the constitution; topic ownership (founder vs elected vs appointed); reputation-gated mod privileges; reporting, removal, appeals, the audit log; AI-assisted triage (the ai topic prices it; you place it in the flow). The rule that keeps it from becoming authoritarian, written as a sentence a user would accept.
5. **Topic creation governance.** Suggest → review → publish; duplicate merge/redirect; the typo problem; who may create a top-level topic vs a subtopic; what an MVP can do with ONE moderator (the owner).
6. **Badges.** What earns one, how many kinds, where they show, the line between "not childish" and Duolingo; the owner will supply designs — specify the slots, not the art.
7. **The MVP.** The smallest progression system that is honest: what is tracked, what is shown, what is gated. A §33 record for the one hard-to-reverse choice (probably the level scale or the reputation formula).

## Challenge

Academic numbering. Levels on every topic. Reputation as one number. Community voting for anything in the MVP. That gamification helps learning at all — bring the evidence both ways.

## Numbers to bring back (url + date)

Stack Overflow's privilege thresholds (three of them); Reddit's mod-revolt subreddit count; Wikipedia's active-editor decline figures; Duolingo's published streak/retention claim; one peer-reviewed effect size for gamification in learning (with its confidence interval if given).
