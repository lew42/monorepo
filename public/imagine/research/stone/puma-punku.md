# Puma Punku's H-blocks

**What's there.** Puma Punku is a T-shaped terraced platform (167 x 117m) at the Tiwanaku complex in Bolivia, part of a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/567/). Its famous interlocking H-shaped blocks are cut andesite, 1-2m per side, quarried ~90km away across Lake Titicaca; the platform's largest foundation blocks are red sandstone (one 7.81 x 5.17 x 1.07m, ~131 tonnes), quarried only 10km away. Popular articles often call the stone "diorite" or "granite" — it isn't. A 2023 Bayesian radiocarbon study of 102 dates puts construction at [AD 580-720](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0288798), replacing an old, now-discredited 1580 BC estimate.

**How mainstream archaeology explains it.** Jean-Pierre Protzen and Stella Nair spent years doing hands-on replication, published as [*The Stones of Tiahuanaco*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Stones_of_Tiahuanaco.html?id=kWmRDwAAQBAJ) (2013). Using only hammerstones — flint, quartzite, obsidian; bronze proved nearly useless against andesite — plus sand-and-water abrasion, they reproduced flat surfaces and right angles to roughly 1mm tolerance. That's the documented Tiwanaku-era toolkit, the same one used at Inca sites. It's a genuinely solid result — with one honest gap: even Nair's own experiments couldn't fully reproduce the flawless interior faces of the H-blocks' deep interlocking recesses in a comparable time. She called it an open, detail-level mystery, not evidence of impossibility.

**The alternative claim.** Two distinct fringe claims circulate. The *Ancient Aliens* line says the surfaces are "laser-flat" and therefore impossible for a Bronze Age culture — a claim with no physical evidence (no machining residue, no out-of-place tool marks) behind it. Separately, materials scientist Joseph Davidovits — who coined the term "geopolymer" — [published SEM/petrographic papers](https://www.geopolymer.org/archaeology/tiahuanaco-monuments-tiwanaku-pumapunku-bolivia/) arguing the sandstone and andesite are cast, man-made concrete, not carved natural stone. The materials-science methodology passed peer review in *Materials Letters*; the archaeological interpretation is rejected by mainstream archaeologists and geologists, who point to identified quarry sites, visible tool marks, and unfinished blocks in place.

**Open question.** No stone-tool cache has ever been excavated and positively tied to Puma Punku's construction, and nobody has directly observed how blocks up to 131 tonnes were transported and lifted with no wheel or draft animal in the archaeological record. The method is a well-informed model, not an observed process.

**What would settle it.** A found tool cache or an unfinished-block sequence at the site with matching hammer marks would close the mainstream account's last gap. A complete H-block, replicated end-to-end under strictly period tools in a realistic timeframe, would directly test the 1mm claim. And independent, blind replication of Davidovits's SEM data by outside geology labs — rather than leaving it as one research group's result — would actually test the geopolymer claim instead of just disputing it.
