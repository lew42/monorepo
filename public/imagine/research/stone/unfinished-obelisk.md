# The unfinished obelisk's tool marks

**What's there.** In the Aswan quarry lies a granite obelisk abandoned mid-extraction: [41.75m long](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/59320/59320-h/59320-h.htm), 4.2m at the base, an estimated ~1,168 tons if finished — figures from Reginald Engelbach's original 1922 survey (secondary sources round to "1,090-1,200 tons"). Engelbach catalogued numerous fissures (labeled a through roughly p) that appeared as quarrying progressed; the cumulative cracking made the block unworkable, and it was abandoned still attached to bedrock on its underside. He recovered dolerite pounding balls in quantity — 9-15lb, 8-13 inches across — and a later study catalogued 1,419 of them from the site, mean diameter 14.2cm. Attribution to Hatshepsut (c.1508-1458 BCE) is stylistic, not inscriptional: there's no royal cartouche on the obelisk itself.

**How mainstream archaeology explains it.** Workers pounded a trench (~75cm wide) around the block to isolate it, then would have undercut it. A [2023 photogrammetric re-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212054823000292) of Engelbach's data estimates ~130 artisans working shoulder-to-shoulder could reach 3.4m trench depth in ~3.5 months — broadly matching Engelbach's own century-old labor estimate. Newer work adds a wrinkle: quarry surveys have found charcoal, ash and burnt mud-brick layers suggesting fire-setting supplemented pounding, dramatically speeding trench-cutting in replication tests — refining, not overturning, the mainstream account. Separately, [tool-mark microscopy](https://rivista.museoegizio.it/article/reading-tool-marks-on-egyptian-stone-sculpture/) using Reflectance Transformation Imaging shows percussive marks form as shallow, irregular pits distinct from the parallel striations sawing leaves — evidence read as confirming pounding, not sawing.

**The alternative claim.** Machinist-author Christopher Dunn argues the trench walls show [unusually even, regular striations](https://techzelle.com/aswan-quarry/) he says are inconsistent with hand-pounding, proposing an unspecified machine with a pivoting arm; Brien Foerster has separately floated "vibrational technology." Neither has published a point-by-point comparison against actual pounding-replica surfaces — the comparison mainstream tool-mark studies (like Serotta's RTI work) exist to make, but haven't yet been aimed directly at Dunn's specific claim.

**Open question.** What fraction of the trench used fire-setting versus pounding alone, and does that ratio match wear patterns on the recovered pounders? And can the Hatshepsut attribution be tied to direct textual evidence rather than stylistic inference from her Karnak obelisk program? Round 2 also found the 2023 photogrammetric paper cited both a ~1,234 cm³/worker-hour figure and, elsewhere, 216 cm³/hour at 85 hits/minute for the same trench — whether that's one number in different units or a real discrepancy couldn't be confirmed past the paywall.

**What would settle it.** A like-for-like RTI or 3D micro-topography comparison of the actual trench walls against fresh dolerite-pounding and fire-setting replicas, aimed specifically at Dunn's "even striation" claim; chemical/soot residue dating across more trench sections to quantify fire-setting's real extent; and any inscriptional find naming a pharaoh at this specific quarry face.
