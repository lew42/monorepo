# The Serapeum of Saqqara's granite boxes

**What's there.** Beneath Saqqara, [Auguste Mariette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serapeum_of_Saqqara) rediscovered the Serapeum's underground galleries in 1850-52, recognizing a buried sphinx avenue from a description in Strabo. The Greater Vaults hold 24 surviving stone sarcophagi — granite, basalt, and diorite, not one uniform stone — of which only 4 carry inscriptions: Amasis II (c.570-526 BC), Cambyses II (c.530 BC), Khabash (c.336 BC), and a blank Ptolemaic-era box. The famous "70-100 ton, 4 x 2.3 x 3.3m" figure repeated across tourism sites overstates the one well-documented box Wikipedia's Mariette/Dodson-sourced synthesis actually gives: 3.85 x 2.32m external, roughly 62 tonnes body-plus-lid. Most of the undecorated boxes are dated by nearby pottery and stelae, not inscriptions on the boxes themselves — a real methodological gap, though no evidence supports the Old Kingdom date some fringe writers claim.

**How mainstream archaeology explains it.** Denys Stocks (University of Manchester) reconstructed period-accurate copper saws, tube drills, bow drills, and dolerite pounders and used them on real granite, matching tool-mark profiles, wear patterns, and drill-core shapes to actual artifacts — laid out in [*Experiments in Egyptian Archaeology*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287348456_Experiments_in_Egyptian_archaeology_Stoneworking_technology_in_ancient_Egypt). That's the accepted mechanism. One detail stays genuinely open even within Egyptology: a 1983 [Penn Museum study](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/ancient-egyptian-stone-drilling/) found loose sand alone doesn't reproduce the concentric striations on real drill holes — fixed abrasive does — and concluded "we cannot say for sure which one was used."

**The alternative claim.** Aerospace engineer Christopher Dunn measured Serapeum box interiors in 1995 with a 0.0002-inch precision straightedge and found them flat, then in 2001 found lid-to-wall squareness within 0.00005 inch, plus ~4mm corner radii he calls functionally pointless — [writing that](https://gizapower.com/gizeh/articles/lost-technologies-of-ancient-egypt-adaptation/) hand tools "would not produce the precision of a Model T Ford," implying lost machine technology without naming what it was. A [Metabunk forum analysis](https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-ancient-egyptians-could-not-work-granite-without-high-tech-diamond-tools.12963/) counters that granite is *ground*, not cut — sand or emery carried in soft copper does the work over long labor-time — and catches a specific math error in a widely-shared fringe pressure calculation (treating a hollow drill tube as solid, overstating force ~15x). But mainstream and fringe are arguing past each other: Stocks-style replication proves period tools *could* make marks like these, which isn't the same claim as re-measuring Dunn's actual flatness numbers — a distinction [one direct analysis](https://www.neuralgrimoire.com/the-giza-power-plant-theory/) draws explicitly.

**Round 2 dug for the missing re-measurement directly — and found why it's missing.** Access isn't the reason: the Serapeum was closed for structural conservation from the early 2000s and [reopened to tourists in 2012](https://phys.org/news/2012-09-egypt-reopens-historic-serapeum-saqqara.html), and a 2026 peer-reviewed laser survey proved [the right instruments are already active at Saqqara](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/8/1138) — just never pointed at these 24 boxes. What exists instead: a 2021 hobbyist iPhone LiDAR scan of one box, [explicit about not being metrology-grade](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/saqqara-serapeum-box-1-cbd2f61dd96a47e5b9b2a2fd9f46bf0a); an April 2019 amateur French exploration channel that laser-telemetered four boxes before security cut them off, [landing on a third independent weight figure](https://www.theancientconnection.com/megaliths/egypt/the-serapeum-of-saqqara/) for box 24 (39.77 tonnes) — lower than both the tourist-site "70-100 ton" claim and Wikipedia's ~62-tonne figure, but still just linear dimensions, not flatness; and, as of 2026, [UnchartedX videos claiming a new laser scan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LcY19FjCErY) with no raw data, team, or methodology released. One AI-search summary during this round even invented a named "Digital Saqqara Project" with a specific tolerance figure — [directly checked and confirmed fabricated](https://techzelle.com/serapeum/), not present on any page it cited.

**The Dunn answer.** No independent, methodologically transparent, instrument-based re-measurement of Dunn's specific boxes exists, from either direction, as of 2026. It would take a university archaeometry lab or heritage-scanning team — the kind already scanning Saqqara's pyramids — running a coordinate-measuring-machine or structured-light survey of a few boxes' interior corners, with the raw point cloud published for open scrutiny, the way UnchartedX did once for an unrelated vase (see the predynastic-vases page) and let real metrologists argue over actual numbers instead of narrative.

**What would settle it.** That same blind, instrument-verified re-measurement, published with raw data; tool-mark microscopy comparing box interiors to Stocks-style replica surfaces; and a full quarry-to-box petrographic sourcing survey for all 24 boxes, rather than the single assumed-Aswan-granite story.
