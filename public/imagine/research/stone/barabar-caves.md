# The Barabar caves' polished interiors

**What's there.** The four [Barabar Hill caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barabar_Caves) — Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Karan Chaupar, Vishwakarma — are hewn from solid granite/granitic gneiss. Sudama's outer hall is 9.98 x 5.94m leading to a ~6m semi-hemispherical inner chamber; Lomas Rishi is similar but was left structurally unfinished and carries no dedication. Brahmi inscriptions naming Ashoka ("Piyadasi") date Sudama and Vishwakarma to the 12th year of his reign (c.261-250 BCE); his grandson Dasharatha Maurya dedicated three more caves on nearby Nagarjuni Hill around 230 BCE. Interiors carry the famous glass-like "Mauryan polish," also seen on Ashoka's stone pillars. In 2022 the [ASI proposed](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/asi-to-seek-world-heritage-status-for-barabar-and-nagarjuni-caves-in-bihar-436188/) the whole cluster for UNESCO's tentative list.

**How mainstream archaeology explains it.** Excavator John Marshall called the workmanship's precision "extraordinary," and archaeologists attribute the polish to prolonged abrasive grinding and burnishing — fine quartz or emery grains, agate burnishers — possibly influenced by Achaemenid Persian stoneworking. Here's the honest part: [Wikipedia's own account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_polish) calls the *exact* mechanism "one of the genuine unsolved problems of ancient technology." No experimental study has identified the specific abrasive or timed a full replication — this gap is real and acknowledged by mainstream sources themselves, not a fringe talking point.

**The alternative claim.** Alternative-history creator [UnchartedX published 3D laser scans](https://unchartedx.com/site/2024/07/10/barabar-presented-by-unchartedx/) of the interiors calling the flatness "utterly incredible precision," framed toward lost-technology speculation without naming a specific mechanism. A [post on a Graham Hancock forum](https://grahamhancock.com/phorum/read.php?1,1335816) goes further, citing an uncited scanning outfit's claim of 0.1-degree angle tolerance and ~2.5mm flatness, quoting stonemasons who call replication "nearly impossible." None of these numbers have been published anywhere peer-reviewed or independently verified.

**Open question.** Lomas Rishi's dating is itself disputed — archaeologist S.P. Gupta argued its chaitya-arch facade actually postdates Ashoka, possibly carved under the empire's last ruler before work stopped at the Sunga takeover (~185 BCE). And nobody, mainstream or fringe, has published a residue or tool-mark study identifying what was actually dragged across this granite to polish it.

**What would settle it.** Controlled replication — polishing local granite with only Mauryan-period iron tools plus candidate abrasives (quartz sand, emery, cloth/leather burnishers), timed and measured against the originals — combined with tool-mark microscopy of the real chisel and polish marks. That would either confirm the hand-abrasive hypothesis or show it genuinely can't reach Barabar's flatness in plausible labor-time, which is the actual open question mainstream archaeology admits it hasn't closed.
