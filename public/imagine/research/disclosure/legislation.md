# Congress: NDAA Provisions and the Records Act

**The bill.** In 2023, Sens. Chuck Schumer and Mike Rounds filed the [UAP Disclosure Act](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-rounds-introduce-new-legislation-to-declassify-government-records-related-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-and-ufos_modeled-after-jfk-assassination-records-collection-act--as-an-amendment-to-ndaa) as an NDAA amendment, explicitly modeled on the JFK Assassination Records Collection Act of 1962. As written it would have granted the government eminent domain over any "recovered technologies of unknown origin" held privately, and created an independent UAP Records Review Board to arbitrate declassification. *(established)*

**What actually passed, FY2024.** Both the eminent-domain clause and the independent review board were stripped in conference. What survived, per [legal analysis](https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2024/01/implications-of-the-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap-amendment-in-the-2024-national-defense-authorization-act-ndaa/), was a National Archives-managed UAP Records Collection, with agencies ordered to review and transfer relevant records by October 2024 — transparency machinery, without the teeth. Some analysts read the cuts as the bill [falling far short](https://briantreese.medium.com/why-the-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap-disclosure-act-of-2023-falls-far-short-of-its-intended-purpose-and-what-that-means-for-transparency-16df8b5e4449) of its authors' stated intent. *(established)*

**Stall, then return — twice.** The Act was excluded entirely from the FY2026 NDAA: on Oct 9 2025 the Senate finalized its FY2026 agreement without it, and since the House had already passed its own bill with no UAP language, the provision never even reached conference — a third consecutive year comprehensive UAP transparency legislation was blocked or weakened. On July 22 2026 the House [adopted Rep. Eric Burlison's version](https://burlison.house.gov/media/press-releases/house-adopts-burlison-amendment-establishing-uap-disclosure-framework) for FY2027 instead — eminent domain restored, plus a permanent Records Collection, an independent Review Board, and disclosure duties extended to government contractors. Separately, Schumer, Rounds and Gillibrand [refiled their own Senate version](https://www.congress.gov/amendment/119th-congress/senate-amendment/3111/text), now with language explicitly stating the Act creates no additional access to classified Special Access Programs — a direct answer to the objection that helped sink eminent domain in FY2024 conference. Both chambers have now moved a version before conference for the first time in three tries; the [FY2024 precedent](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12704) is still that the strongest provisions are exactly the ones cut at that stage. *(established, outcome pending)*

**The administrative side moved faster than the law.** Ahead of that fight, ODNI issued [2026 preliminary guidance](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/08/odni-issues-preliminary-guidance-on-disclosure-of-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena) letting intelligence-community staff report UAP information to a designated task-force representative, free of old non-disclosure agreements — but explicitly not authorizing disclosure to the public or to Congress outside existing channels. That is real machinery, and it is also proof the executive branch is not waiting on the NDAA fight to make its own, narrower move. See [PURSUE](./pursue-disclosure/) for the fuller executive-order thread this guidance sits inside. *(established)*

**What would settle it.** The FY2027 NDAA's final, signed text: does eminent domain plus an independent review board survive conference this time — with both chambers now on record supporting some version, for the first time — or is history about to repeat FY2024's pattern of a strong bill entering and a records-collection shell leaving?
