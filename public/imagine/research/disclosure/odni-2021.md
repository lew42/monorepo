# The 2021 ODNI Assessment

**The document.** On June 25 2021, mandated by the FY2021 Intelligence Authorization Act, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence published its ["Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena"](https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/Prelimary-Assessment-UAP-20210625.pdf) — the first unclassified USG accounting of the modern UAP dataset, covering incidents from Nov 2004 to Mar 2021. *(established)*

**The headline number.** Of 144 incidents reviewed, only one was resolved — a large deflating balloon. The other 143 were sorted into five open explanatory bins: airborne clutter, natural atmospheric phenomena, USG or industry programs, foreign adversary systems, and an unlabeled "other." That "143 unexplained" figure is the single most-repeated statistic in the whole disclosure arc. *(established)*

**The report's own caveat.** Read past the headline and ODNI attributes most of that unexplained rate to inconsistent sensor data and the total absence of a standardized reporting mechanism across the services — not to a positive case for novel phenomena. The report explicitly says better data collection, not more speculation, is the next step. This caveat is the part advocacy retellings usually drop. *(established)*

**Reading the gap two ways.** The advocacy reading: 99% percent unexplained is itself the finding — a persistent, uncatalogued phenomenon the government cannot identify. The skeptical reading: "unexplained" here means "insufficient data to classify," a bar so low that ordinary drones, balloons, and sensor artifacts routinely clear it, as the 2024 [Starlink-train misidentification case study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08155) later demonstrated for a similar unclassified incident. Both readings are consistent with the same 143 number. *(contested)*

**What would settle it.** Retroactive application of the standardized, multi-sensor reporting the report itself calls for, run against the 143 unresolved cases specifically — largely still outstanding five years on, folded instead into AARO's growing, separately-tracked backlog.
