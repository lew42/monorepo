# Two "Resolved" Cases, Read Side by Side

Round 1 named entities without walking one case end to end. Here are two AARO calls it out of hundreds — one solid, one contested — because together they show what "resolved" actually spans in the government's own record.

**The jellyfish, Al Taqaddum, Oct 2017 — high confidence.** A force-protection aerostat over Al Taqaddum Air Base, Iraq, recorded 17 minutes of infrared video of an object that looked, once released by Jeremy Corbell in Jan 2024, like a jellyfish pulsing against the sky. [AARO's published case resolution report](https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_Al_Taqaddam_Case_Resolution_Final.pdf) assesses it as a cluster of partially and fully inflated balloons, no anomalous performance, with a stated methodology anyone can check: full-motion video plus pixel-level examination of shape and translucency against known balloon-cluster imagery, and geolocation data from the aerostat itself used to derive the object's speed and drift — which matched local wind, not powered flight. *(established)*

**Eglin, Jan 26 2023 — moderate confidence.** A pilot near Eglin AFB reported four cone-topped objects, roughly 12 feet across, at 16,000ft, alongside a radar malfunction. [AARO's report](https://www.theblackvault.com/documentarchive/aaro-releases-final-report-on-eglin-uap-incident/) calls it a lighter-than-air object, most likely a commercial lighting balloon — moderate confidence, backed by lab tests reproducing the described shape, explicitly stating no anomalous flight characteristics were confirmed. *(established)*

**Why Eglin does not actually close.** [Marik von Rennenkampff](https://thedebrief.org/aaro-has-only-moderate-confidence-in-findings-from-its-latest-uap-investigation-its-easy-to-see-why/) reported the balloon manufacturer itself says its balloons do not come untethered randomly. Mick West called the object a white sphere wearing a hat and said its irregular thermal signature does not fit the lighting-balloon read. Neither AARO's report nor its critics explain how four objects held a diamond formation, or why the aircraft's camera and a known-faulty radar circuit breaker both failed on the same sortie. AARO's own "moderate confidence" label is the tell — it is not the same epistemic weight as Al Taqaddum's "high confidence." *(contested)*

**The lesson.** AARO's often-cited 114-of-319 resolution rate reads as one uniform standard of proof. It is not. It spans a real gradient: a call built on geolocation and wind data that nobody has seriously challenged, down to a call with a named on-record critic saying the physical evidence still does not fit. Reading the backlog as binary — resolved vs. mystery — hides that gradient, and it is the same gradient that runs through every case in this whole arc, from [the Navy videos](./aatip-videos/) to [Grusch](./grusch-aaro/) to the still-open [Virginia maritime case](./state-of-play/). *(speculation — how much of the 114 sits at Al Taqaddum's end vs. Eglin's is not published, and nobody outside AARO can check it without the same case-by-case reports)*

**What would settle it.** AARO publishing a confidence label, not just a one-word "resolved," for every closed case in its running total — so an outside reader could tell a jellyfish from an Eglin without filing separate requests for each case resolution report one at a time.
