# Why the shapes recur — the mainstream comparative frame

Every subject in this program shares a handful of visual traits: round or oversized heads, radiating lines, huge eyes, haloed outlines. It's tempting to read the repetition itself as evidence of one shared source. Art history and rock-art research have their own answer, and it doesn't need contact between cultures — or with visitors.

**Radiating heads: convergent solar symbolism.** A luminous disc or radiating crown around a divine head shows up independently in [ancient Egyptian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_(religious_iconography)), in the *khvarenah* of Zoroastrian Persia associated with Mithra by the 300s BCE, around Helios and Apollo in Greek art, in rayed aureoles on Indus-valley Mohenjo-daro pottery from around 2000 BCE, and in Buddhist art from Gandhara by the 1st century CE — centuries before it entered Christian art in the 4th century. [Art historians read this](https://albertis-window.com/2013/09/history-of-the-halo-in-art/) as multiple societies independently reaching for the same visual solution: the sun is the one universally shared, intuitively understood reference for divine radiance. No borrowed image required. *(established)*

**Geometric trance imagery: shared neurology, not shared symbols.** For the more abstract recurring shapes in rock art — grids, zigzags, dots, spirals — David Lewis-Williams and Thomas Dowson's 1988 [neuropsychological model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis-Williams) proposes these are entoptic phenomena: patterns the human nervous system generates under altered states of consciousness, common to any human brain regardless of culture. *(established, but not uncontested — see below)*

**The model has real critics.** Archaeologist Paul Bahn and psychologist Patricia Helvenston [argue](https://dead-but-dreaming.com/2024/09/22/the-entoptic-model-bridging-ancient-art-and-consciousness/) that projecting documented modern shamanic practice back tens of thousands of years onto Palaeolithic art is methodologically shaky, however neurologically plausible the mechanism. Their original doubt that psychotropic plants existed in Palaeolithic Europe is now generally considered wrong — native psilocybin species did — but the deeper question, how far ethnographic analogy can travel across that much time, is a live argument inside the field, not a fringe objection. *(contested)*

**What a fair reader keeps.** Two independent, mainstream, non-alien frameworks — solar convergence and entoptic neurology — already do the explanatory work the ancient-astronaut genre claims only contact can do. Nobody has published a single table testing every depiction in this program (Val Camonica, Wandjina, Tassili, the Nazca figures, Pacal) against both frameworks at once; that comparison, not another site-by-site rebuttal, is what would actually settle why the shapes recur.
