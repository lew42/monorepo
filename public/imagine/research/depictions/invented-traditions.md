# The recency signature — three more "ancient" traditions that aren't

**The pattern round 1 found.** The Hopi "Blue Star Kachina" prophecy, widely cited as ancient forewarning of a celestial event, has no documented version predating the late 20th century — a modern text retrofitted onto real Hopi ceremonial tradition. That raised an obvious question: is this a one-off, or a recognizable move? *(established)*

**Dogon Sirius knowledge.** French ethnographers Marcel Griaule and Germaine Dieterlen reported in the 1930s–1950s that Dogon cosmology described Sirius as a double star with a dense, invisible companion — the basis of Robert Temple's 1976 *The Sirius Mystery*, which argues [only alien contact](https://www.badarchaeology.com/extraterrestrials/the-sirius-mystery/) could explain it. In 1991, anthropologist Walter van Beek returned to check it and [found no one](https://skepdic.com/dogon.html) — including Griaule's own original informants — who had heard of Sirius as a double star; several said they'd learned it from Griaule. The mundane channel is itself contested: Carl Sagan proposed a [knowledgeable European visitor](https://chandra.harvard.edu/chronicle/0400/sirius_part2.html) in the 1930s–40s; astronomer Noah Brosch instead points to an [1893 French eclipse expedition](https://spokenpast.com/articles/dogon-sirius-mystery/) that spent five weeks in the region; journalist James Oberg cites 1920s newspaper coverage of Sirius B. All three would explain the outcome; which one actually happened is still unsettled. *(established / contested)*

**The Emerald Tablets of Thoth.** Maurice Doreal claimed to have found and translated ancient tablets during a 1925 Giza trip — but [the text wasn't published](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Tablets_of_Thoth_the_Atlantean) until the 1940s or early 1950s, and researcher Jason Colavito has traced passages [copied nearly verbatim](https://www.jasoncolavito.com/the-emerald-tablet.html) from H.P. Lovecraft and Frank Belknap Long stories published in 1929 — the same years Doreal was supposedly still sitting on an untranslated ancient manuscript. *(established)*

**The Kolbrin Bible.** Presented as eleven Egyptian and Celtic manuscripts saved from a real 1184 Glastonbury Abbey fire and hidden for eight centuries, the [documented paper trail](https://hejon07.substack.com/p/the-kolbrin-bible) actually starts in 1992, with an unnamed reconstructor and an original that was never preserved or shown to anyone; a 2005–2006 US edition briefly inserted the modern term "Nibiru" before quietly removing it. The name even echoes [Coelbren y Beirdd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iolo_Morganwg), an alphabet a Welsh poet invented and presented as ancient Druidic writing two centuries ago — manufactured antiquity has a home-grown precedent. *(established)*

**What a fair reader keeps.** All four cases share one shape: a claim of deep antiquity, a first documentable appearance clustered tightly in the 20th century, and content that mirrors whatever sources were circulating right around that appearance. None of these needed a confession or a forgery trial to be caught — the dates alone are the tell. That's a useful diagnostic to carry into the next "ancient tradition" someone cites. *(contested)*

**Round 4 checked whether the gap alone is the tell — it isn't.** Genuinely ancient material carries the same recency gap: the Iliad is set in the 12th-century-BC Bronze Age collapse but composed around the 8th century BC, and the Rigveda was transmitted purely orally for over a thousand years before its earliest surviving manuscript. Both pass a check the four cases above never faced — Homer's oral-formulaic meter and the Rigveda's cross-checkable dual recitation are independent evidence of real transmission, unconnected to either text's content. King Arthur is the harder case: Geoffrey of Monmouth's 1136 *Historia* invents a source and mirrors 12th-century Norman politics exactly like the four cases above, yet Arthur is attested three and two centuries earlier in the *Historia Brittonum* and the *Annales Cambriae* — a thin real antecedent trail these four cases have none of, even though historians still mostly reject a historical Arthur. **Revised diagnostic:** it's the gap *plus* zero independent evidence of anything predating the claim's public surfacing — still four for four here, now a two-part test instead of one. *(contested)*
