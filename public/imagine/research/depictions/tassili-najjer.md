# Tassili n'Ajjer — the "Great Martian God" of Sefar

**The artifact.** In the 1950s, French archaeologist Henri Lhote surveyed the Tassili n'Ajjer plateau (Algeria) and named a towering, featureless-headed figure at Sefar/Jabbaren the "Great Martian God" — an evocative nickname for its uncanny appearance, part of the Round Head style of Central Saharan rock art. *(established)*

**Dating: genuinely messy, then genuinely resolved.** Early estimates for the Round Head style [ranged wildly](https://inference-review.com/article/central-saharan-rock-art), from about 10,000 to over 20,000 years — dates prehistorian Jean-Loïc Le Quellec calls poorly argued. Alfred Muzzolini's reassessment, refined with cartographic and phylogenetic methods, moved specialists to a much narrower and now broadly accepted range of roughly 7500–5500 BCE. *(established)*

**What archaeology says it depicts.** Read via analogy to later sub-Saharan African ritual practice, the [Round Head figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Head_Period) are masked ancestral or ceremonial beings tied to initiation rites — not a unique reading invented to rebut aliens, but the same kind of ethnographic-analogy reading used across African rock art generally. *(established)*

**The alien interpretation.** Popular [ancient-astronaut sources](https://www.thegypsythread.org/ancient-astronauts-tassili-najjer/) describe the Sefar figure's featureless head and full-body covering as gloves, a helmet and a spacesuit, arguing people would only paint what they directly observed — treating Lhote's descriptive nickname as though it were physical evidence. *(fringe)*

**What a fair reader keeps.** This is the honest nuance, not a clean win for either side: the *dating* is genuinely settled now, but the *identification* still rests entirely on analogy — comparing a 7500-year-old image to ritual practices documented thousands of years later and a continent's width away. That's a real evidentiary gap, just a much smaller and more ordinary one than "aliens vs. archaeology" — closer to the kind of gap every deep-antiquity artifact without its own text has (unlike Pacal's named glyphs, or Nazca's excavated ritual pottery). *(contested)*
