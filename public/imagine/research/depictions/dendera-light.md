# The Dendera reliefs — "light bulb" or lotus-birth?

**The artifact.** Carved in underground crypts of the [Temple of Hathor at Dendera](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/dendera-light) (Ptolemaic/Roman era, roughly 54 BCE–68 CE), the reliefs show an oval, bulb-like shape containing a snake, flanked by Djed-pillar-shaped arms and a kneeling figure. *(established)*

**What Egyptology says.** Egyptologists read the oval as the *hn*-vessel, likened to the womb of the sky goddess Nut, and the snake as the god [Harsomtus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendera_light) (sometimes merged with Ra) being born from a lotus flower — a standard creation/resurrection motif. The Djed pillar (stability) and serpent iconography appear identically elsewhere in Egyptian temple art with no "bulb" reading required. *(established)*

**The alien/ancient-technology interpretation.** A Norwegian engineer first floated an electric-lamp reading; Austrian authors Peter Krassa and Rainer Habeck [popularized it in a dedicated book](https://www.ancient-origins.net/ancient-technology/dendera-light-0081), following Erich von Daniken's broader "ancient high technology" framing — reading the oval as a bulb, the snake as a filament, and the supports as a cable. *(fringe)*

**What a fair reader keeps.** Archaeologist Kenneth Feder's rebuttal is a clean falsification test, and it's a strong one: an electrical lighting technology used across a temple complex for centuries should leave sockets, glass shards, filament wire, or telltale soot-absence patterns — none exist, and Dendera's own walls carry ordinary soot-blackened lamp niches exactly where you'd expect open flame. This case is closer to closed than most on this list. *(contested)*

**What would settle it.** It's already close to settled by the total absence of supporting material culture (wiring, batteries, bulbs) anywhere in the Egyptian archaeological record. The remaining open thread is purely historiographic: dating the original Norwegian engineer's publication to pin down exactly when the "lamp" reading entered circulation.
