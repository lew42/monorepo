# Pacal's sarcophagus — astronaut or World Tree?

**The artifact.** The carved limestone [sarcophagus lid](https://www.worldhistory.org/Kinich_Janaab_Pacal/) covers the tomb of K'inich Janaab' Pakal (603–683 CE), ruler of Palenque, discovered in 1952 in the Temple of the Inscriptions. It shows a reclining figure at the base of a cross-shaped tree topped by a bird. *(established)*

**What Maya epigraphy says.** Deciphered Maya iconography reads the panel as [Pakal's death and rebirth](https://mayan.org/mysteries/pakal-tomb-lid-decipherment/) through the World Tree (Wakah-Chan): he falls into the underworld at death and rises toward the heavens along the tree, flanked by the Celestial Bird above and the Sun Monster / underworld jaws below. The "controls" are stylized Maya hand gestures, the "exhaust" is the tree's roots (a near-mandatory element of World Tree art), and the "breathing tube" is a bone nose-ornament used in funerary dress. Classic Maya writing on the tomb itself names Pakal and dates his death. *(established)*

**The alien interpretation.** [Erich von Daniken's](http://alienexplorations.blogspot.com/2012/02/von-danikens-point-of-view.html) *Chariots of the Gods?* (1968) described the panel as a man operating a rocket's controls, leaning forward with an oxygen hose, flames issuing from an exhaust pipe — arguing that "primitive" imagination couldn't have produced an image so close to a modern astronaut. *(fringe)*

**What a fair reader keeps.** Because the World Tree motif recurs on dozens of other Maya monuments with no "machine" reading ever needed, and because the tomb's own glyphs already identify the occupant, [Mayanists](https://www.ancientaliensdebunked.com/pacal-s-rocket) treat this case less as a live debate and more as a demonstration of the argument's method: pattern-matching an image to modern technology while ignoring text that was, by the time of Daniken's writing, largely undeciphered but has since become broadly readable. *(contested)*

**What would settle it.** It already is settled, by the deciphered glyphs naming Pakal and his lineage directly on the lid. The honest remaining question is rhetorical, not evidentiary — why the rocket reading still circulates decades after Maya script became widely readable in the 1980s–90s.
